UI plan · 23 October 2026 · the farm, mill and roastery screens

A map-first screen

The plan is to make the farm, the mill and the roastery fill the phone, with the farm switcher at the top, the menu buttons at the bottom, and every panel sliding up over the map instead of pushing it away. The taller map is then room to grow each farm, the mill yard and the roastery. Figures come from the current build measured on an iPhone 14 screen (390 × 844) and from our playtest reviews.

Built · 23 October 2026

What shipped

Your call was to keep today’s menu styling and every tab’s content, and to open the tabs as half-screen sheets that put away when you tap the tab again. Those screenshots are from the build now on the main branch and in the playable Beju Farm page.

  • Layout. No page scroll. On a 390 × 844 phone the map is 358 × 656 (78% of the screen), drawn at a crisp 2×. The tabs keep their look, dots and locks.
  • Sheets. Every tab opens the same way, with the same header (icon, name, ▾), and pages no longer repeat the tab name. On short phones the sheet starts a little higher so at least 320 px shows. Scrolling a sheet takes it to full screen below the farm bar. Pull down from the top or drag its handle to go back to half (added after playtest feedback that half a screen was too small to scroll in).
  • Guide. With a sheet open, Rosa’s guide bar moves into the sheet, so the map above stays clear. A walk to a button further down scrolls the sheet to it. A map spot hidden under the sheet points at the tab first.
  • Taller art (192 × 352). Six farms, the mill and five roastery interiors were redrawn with the new space filled. Estate projects sit exactly where they did. Tapping a building or empty lot opens Farm › Buildings.
  • Visits show the top 264 rows (fields and farmyard, or roasters and counter), so the visit window still fits on one screen.
  • Found on the way. The foreman used to hold every lot that matched a regular’s request. That could fill the racks and stop the mill for a day right after a prestige (about 1 bot run in 6). He now keeps one lot back, and the rest sell. Prestige timing is unchanged.

Today, and where it goes

The mockups are drawn to scale for a 390 × 844 screen and use the game's own scene art. The new land in the third phone is a placeholder for art that doesn't exist yet.

What's wrong with today's screen

  • The map is half the screen. On an iPhone 14 the farm card takes 73 px, the map 428 px, the tabs 46 px, and the panel starts at 591 px. The art is drawn at 342 px wide, an uneven 1.78× scale, so some pixels come out wider than others.
  • Panels push the map away. The Fields panel alone runs to 1,160 px, so the page is 1,790 px tall. Scrolling down to hire, build or plant takes the farm off screen, and with it the feedback: cherries landing, lots drying, golden bushes.
  • The buttons sit in the middle. The eight tabs are at 537 px, halfway down the screen and out of easy thumb reach. Thumbs reach the bottom third of a phone most easily.
  • The map is where the game happens. The prestige 5 playtest found 96–100% of actions in Farm, Mill or Roastery at every stage, and early on 62% in Farm › Fields alone. Unlock, Daily, Ranks and Shop are visited a few times a session.
  • There's no room to grow. Each scene is 192 × 240 pixels and already full. The mill shows at most 16 racks with a "+N more" sign, and the roastery has three roaster bays, though the Osaka machiya adds a fourth that isn't drawn.

The layout

Top bar

  • The farm switcher shrinks to one 44 px line: ◀ Mogiana, Brazil ▶, with the weather chip beside the name. The coffee name moves into the Fields sheet.
  • On the Mill and Roastery screens the arrows still change farm, as they do now.

The map

  • It fills the space between the top bar and the bottom buttons, drawn at a whole-number scale: 2× on phones from 360 to 480 px wide, 3× on larger screens. The extra edge is painted in the scene's ground colour.
  • The HUD stays as it is: hearts and the level pill top left, the clock, coins, cranes and store top right.
  • The sound and music buttons move into More › Settings. Toasts sit just above the bottom buttons, off the middle of the farm.
  • Where the world is taller than the screen (small phones, or a farm that has grown), a drag pans it up and down, and Rosa's arrows scroll it to whatever they point at.

Sheets

  • Three heights. Closed (only a "Fields ▴" tab above the buttons), half (the top 40% of the scene stays visible), and full for long lists like the Shop. Drag the handle, or tap the map to close.
  • The content inside is today's panel, so nothing has to be rewritten to start with. Sub-tabs (Fields · Upgrades · Buildings · Staff) sit at the top of the sheet.
  • Pop-up cards (level-ups, Rosa) stay as cards in the middle, above everything.

How it's built

  • Drawing and camera. The game draws into a 192 × 240 frame and copies it up 2×. That becomes a frame as tall as the scene, with a camera offset, copied at the whole-number scale that fits. Taps are converted through the same offset, so bushes, racks and chores keep working.
  • Art. The scenes come from build_sprites.py. Phase B moves the field rows and building lots down and paints the new ground. Phase C draws what fills it. Farm visits render the taller scene too.
  • Rosa's arrows already find map targets by scene coordinates. They gain one rule: scroll the camera first if the target is off screen. The bottom buttons and sheets get new selectors.
  • Larger screens. Tablets and desktop get a centred phone-width column at first. A side-by-side layout (map left, sheet right) can come later.
  • Checks. The playtest bot and the browser tests click tabs and panels, so they move to the new buttons and sheets in the same change. Every phase is measured at 360 × 780 and 390 × 844, in light and dark rooms, with the tutorial played through.
  • Accessibility. Sheets take keyboard focus when they open and give it back when they close. The buttons keep their labels, and the map keeps its description.

Phases

Phase A · the shell, no new art
  • Full-height layout: a slim top bar, the map at a crisp 2×, six bottom buttons, panels as sheets.
  • Sound buttons into Settings, toasts above the buttons, tests and the bot moved to the new layout.
  • The map is about 57% of the screen with the sheet peeking, up to 81% with it closed. That alone fixes the map scrolling away.
Phase B · taller scenes and the camera
  • All six farms, the mill and the roastery grow to 192 × 352. Existing things move down and the new ground stays empty, with a drag to pan where needed.
  • Farm visits, Rosa's arrows and the estate art are updated for the taller scenes.
Phase C · filling the space
  • One design pass each for farm expansion land, the bigger mill yard and the bigger roastery, priced and paced with the bot like the techniques were.
Phase D · tap the thing
  • Tapping the barn opens storage, the pickers' hut opens hiring, a building lot opens Buildings, and the roaster opens the roast picker.
  • Small chips on the map for what needs you, such as "3 lots dry" or "Contract ready", so the sheets are needed less.

Decisions for you

My recommendation first
  • Bottom buttons: six (Farm, Mill, Roast, Unlock, Daily, More). The alternatives are all eight as small icons, or five with Unlock moved into More.
  • Sheet when you arrive on a scene: closed, with the "Fields ▴" tab showing, so the map is the first thing you see. The alternative is peeking at half height.
  • Scene height: 192 × 352 for every scene, panned on short phones. The alternative is longer farms that you scroll like a mine shaft, which suits bigger expansions but changes the feel more.
  • Order: Phase A on its own first, so you can play the new shell before any art changes.

Risks

  • This touches every screen. Phase A should land in one change with the tutorial, the bot and the browser tests updated together, or old selectors will break silently.
  • Taller scenes mean redrawing all eight scene backgrounds and the estate overlays. Phase B is mostly art time.
  • Panning the map can fight with tapping bushes. A pan should only start after a drag of a few pixels, and only where the world is taller than the screen.
  • Players who know the current layout will need one short "things have moved" card from Rosa.
