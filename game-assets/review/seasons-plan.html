Seasons research · 18 October 2026 · measured with the playtest bot

Keep seasons monthly, but stop ranking them by prestige

A calendar month is the right length for a Beju Farm season. The problem is what the season board counts. Prestige comes in a burst in a player's first month and then almost stops. So under today's ranking every new account beats every veteran. A free 30-tier season track, scored in capped "season stars" and dressed in a monthly Japanese theme, fixes the board and gives everyone something to collect. The season reset never touches the farm: only the score, the track and a light monthly rule start again.

How fast players prestige

I ran the current build for three kinds of player. Each sim started on 1 November with the foreman running between sessions and the bot playing actively (cupping, golden bushes, contracts). The casual run covers three months. The regular and keen runs are slower to simulate, so they are reported as far as they got.

Everyone reaches P5 inside their first month: the keen player in a day or two, the regular player in a week, the casual player in four weeks. After that each prestige needs 8 more levels. Each level past 10 costs 1.22× the last, so a prestige costs about 1.228 ≈ 4.9× the know-how of the one before. Income grows much more slowly than that, so even the keen player stalls two levels short of P6 at the end of week 1. A casual player needs about two months for P6 and would take several for P7.

This is intended. Prestige is the long career, and the crest it unlocks is what shows rank on the all-time board. It also means prestige is the wrong thing to rank a month by.

The season board is broken by design

Current rule: prestiges this month, then know-how this month

A player who installs the game on the 1st scores 5 prestiges by the end of the month. A loyal player at P6 scores 0 or 1. So the newcomer outranks every veteran, however much the veteran plays. Worse, a second account started each month wins the Champion badge every month. Know-how alone is fairer: the casual player earned a steady ~33M a month whether new or not. But it is open-ended, so it hands the board to whoever plays the most hours.

A season score should do three things: treat new and old players the same, reward showing up over grinding, and still let keen players stand out at the top. Daily-capped points do all three. That is how Brawl Stars trophies-plus-pass and Duolingo's weekly XP leagues work, and how Hay Day's derby task cards are capped per day.

Meta's developer guide for Quest suggests a fresh event every 2–4 weeks. Beju Farm already has a 7-day drop event and a weekly order, so a monthly season with a weekly beat inside it lands in that range.

Recommendation: calendar-month seasons

Keep: one season per calendar month, Japan time
  • It fits the loops we already have. The free monthly sample needs 20 check-ins in a calendar month. Subscription renewals pay cranes monthly. The season board already resets monthly. One month means one clock for players to learn.
  • It fits the prestige curve. A casual newcomer's first season is a complete arc from P0 to P5. After that, a month is long enough for a veteran to make visible progress on a big prestige without the season being only about prestige.
  • It's the length the evidence points to. Brawl Stars moved to monthly for retention, Clash Royale runs monthly, and two-month seasons lost players after the pass was done.
  • We can make the art. One themed set of six sprites a month is a realistic pace with build_sprites.py. A weekly season could not carry a cosmetic set.

A weekly beat inside each month

  1. Week 1, theme reveal. New season art on the farm, the track resets, and a "new season" popup shows the six cosmetics on offer.
  2. Week 2, drop event. The existing 7-day drop, picked to suit the season (a Geisha honey drop for the winter lights season, for example). Drop contracts pay bonus stars.
  3. Week 3, mid-season. Regular players finish the track around here. The board race between keen players takes over.
  4. Last 3 days, last harvest. Golden bushes appear twice as often. The banner counts down to the season end.

Launch timing: if the game launches after the 15th of a month, let the first season run to the end of the following month, so nobody's first badge comes from a 10-day season.

What a season reset should touch

Games split into three camps on this. Some wipe everything into a fresh world: Path of Exile leagues, Diablo IV seasons and Old School RuneScape Leagues. Some reset only a score and leave the account alone: Clash Royale trophies, and Brawl Stars ranked. Cosy farming games such as Hay Day and Stardew Valley never wipe at all. The fresh-world games have one thing in common: the main account is never touched. The fresh start happens on a new character or in a separate world, and when the season ends that progress moves into the permanent game.

For Beju Farm: never reset the farm

Why a farm or upgrade reset would hurt this game
  • Prestige is already the reset. It wipes level and know-how, puts the far farms to sleep, and keeps buildings, research, staff, coins and varieties. A forced monthly wipe on top of that resets players twice. It also makes the crest meaningless, because the crest records a career.
  • Real money is in the save. The Osaka garden, non-human characters and building skins are bought with cranes from real coffee orders. Wiping or resetting them would break the promise of the crossplay and lose trust.
  • The free samples depend on lifetime prestige. Huila at P2 up to Turrialba at P5 are one-time server grants. If prestige reset each month, players would either re-grind for nothing or, if the ladder followed them, claim coffee every month.
  • The audience is cosy. In the farming games this audience plays, the farm is never wiped. People come back to the farm they built.
  • The board doesn't need it. Capped season stars already put new and old players on the same footing.

Modification 1: a season rule each month

Each theme brings one mild gameplay twist. It changes how the month plays without taking anything away, much like Pokémon GO's season bonuses. Keep each effect to about 10–15%, aim it at active play, and check it against the playtest bot.

  • Momiji: honey-processed lots sell for 15% more.
  • Winter lights: golden bushes last 45 seconds instead of 30.
  • Tsuyu: rainy weather no longer slows growth.
  • Tsukimi: cupping a lot gives one extra season star (still under the daily cap).

Modification 2: a season plot for the fresh start (after launch)

The sims show the risk a no-wipe game carries. After the first month even keen players are two levels from P6 with nothing new to chase. Path of Exile, Diablo IV and OSRS answer that with a fresh world beside the main one, and players love it. A small version fits Beju Farm:

  • A themed four-bed plot that opens on the 1st, with its own festival tokens. Main-farm research, staff and buildings don't apply there, so a player on day one and a P7 veteran start exactly level.
  • It uses the existing farm, mill and contract code with simpler numbers, so a month on it feels like the first hour of the game again.
  • Its harvest milestones pay season stars. Its output replaces "know-how this month" as the board's tie-break, which makes the top of the board an equal-start race instead of a measure of who has the biggest farm.
  • At month end the plot closes, like a league world. Its tokens convert to stars or coins on the main farm, and the badge records the best harvest.

This is the biggest piece of new work in this plan. I'd launch without it, watch how many players stay in month 2, and build it for the third or fourth season if they drift off once their first P5 is done.

Modification 3: close the season on the server

Today the end-of-season badge is worked out on the player's own device the next time they open the game. By then the live board has moved to the new month, so someone who comes back on the 4th can be shown the wrong rank. The server also overwrites each player's row when they post in the new month, so last month's standings are not kept anywhere. The first request of a new month should save the previous month's final board as seasons/<month>. The game then asks the server for the player's final rank before it shows the badge. This is backend only and can go in now.

Modification 4: two things called "season"

The farm already has seasons: the harvest, rainy and winter chip that changes growth speed. A monthly board season would share the word. Call the monthly event by its theme ("Momiji season", "the Sakura season board") and rename the farm chip to "Weather" so players don't confuse the two.

Season stars: the new season score

Stars come from things a player does on a given real day, with a cap on each, so an hour a day earns nearly as much as six hours. Level-ups give no stars, because newcomers level many times faster than veterans. Prestige gives a few stars, but it no longer decides the board.

The season track

The track is free, has 30 tiers at 15 stars each (450 stars), and gives a cosmetic every 5 tiers. The other tiers give small play items, never cranes. Cranes stay tied to real coffee orders. Stars earned past tier 30 still count on the board.

Rules for seasonal cosmetics

  • Prestige stays the status symbol. Seasonal items sit around the prestige crest and never replace it. A nameplate charm is a small motif pinned to the plate, so a P7 player's crest still reads first on the board.
  • Hats are a new layer. They need a hat slot drawn over every character sprite, human and non-human. This is the one new piece of art tech; the decor, backgrounds and building skins all reuse systems that exist today.
  • Dated items stay exclusive. The season badge and the tier-30 title ("Momiji Harvester 2026") can never be earned again.
  • Other items come back. Twelve months later, a season's decoration, background, hat, skin and charm return in a Season archive in the Shop, for cranes. Missing a month never locks someone out for good, and the archive gives cranes from real orders a steady, cosmetic-only use.
  • Rank rewards stay small. Keep Champion, Top 3 and Top 10 badges, drawn in the season's art. Add an animated plate shimmer for the Top 10 that lasts the following month. Once there are a few hundred players, switch to percentile tiers (top 1%, 10%, 25%) so the rewards still mean something.
  • Collect the year. Earning a badge in all 12 seasons of a year gives a permanent ring around the crest for that year.
  • No paid pass at launch. A free track fits the cosy genre and the coffee brand. Real coffee orders already earn cranes, and cranes buy archive items.

What it would take to build

  1. Stars and caps. S.season gains stars, a per-real-day cap ledger and claimed tiers. The award hooks sit beside the existing task, golden bush, cupping, contract, weekly and prestige code.
  2. A Season sub-tab in Ranks. It shows the track, the days left and the theme preview. The end-of-season popup shows the badge and the tiers reached.
  3. Board and server. Ranking becomes season stars, then know-how. game-score takes seasonStars and checks it against a daily rate limit (about 25 a day plus 10 per prestige). This is backend only, so nothing shows on the live site.
  4. Theme table and sprites. A SEASONS table keyed by month, plus the hat layer and the Momiji, Winter lights and Shōgatsu sets in build_sprites.py.
  5. Season archive in the Shop for cranes, which unlocks a year after each season.
  6. Re-run the sim to tune the caps against the targets above.
  7. Close the season on the server. Save each month's final board and serve final ranks to the end-of-season popup. Backend only.
  8. Season rules in the theme table, checked with the sim. Rename the farm's season chip to Weather.
  9. Later: the season plot, if players drift off in month 2.

Built

Season stars, the 30-tier track, all 12 themes with their art and rules, the Season sub-tab, the Season archive, the stars-first board, the server-side season close and the Weather rename are in the game. They are in the hidden /farm/ build and the backend, and nothing shows on the public site.

The playtest bot, playing 30 minutes a day and catching every golden bush, hit every daily cap: about 19 stars a day, plus 15 a week from the weekly order. That finishes the track around day 21. A real casual player who misses some bushes and tasks should earn 12–15 stars a day and end around tier 24–28.

One change from the plan: only the season's first prestige pays stars. With 10 stars for every prestige, a new player's five quick early prestiges added about 50 stars and put them back ahead of veterans on the board.
