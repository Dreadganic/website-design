Playtest · 17 October 2026 · bot-played to prestige 5

Beju Farm reaches prestige 5 in under three hours

A bot played the current build from a new save to prestige 5, once as an always-on player and once as a casual player with three 10-minute sessions a day. The first prestige lands right where the genre puts it. Everything after it comes far too quickly, because content runs out and the prestige requirement stops growing.

2.75 hof active play to prestige 5

55 minto open Osaka without a single real order

The bot plays well: it decides every 2 seconds, taps ripe shrubs whenever it has stamina, and buys the cheapest useful thing first. A person will be slower, perhaps 1.5 to 2 times, so read every time on this page as a lower bound. The bot ran inside the real game code at about 700 times speed with a fake clock, so real-day features such as check-ins and month changes behaved as they would for a player.

What the run showed

Seven problems

  1. Design New farms stay at one picker. The hire price is shared across all farms and rises 1.5 times per picker, so the 17th picker costs about 65,000 coins. The bot ended with 12 pickers at Mogiana and one at each other farm. New regions add almost nothing.
  2. Design Prestige costs nothing. Because the other farms are so small, putting them to sleep barely dents income. The intended downside does not bite.
  3. Design The requirement stops growing. The level needed rises 10, 12, 14 and so on, but is capped at 20 within a season. Once the farms are built, income reaches about 650,000 know-how an hour, and level 20 needs only 116,000. A dedicated player could prestige every 20 to 30 minutes, dozens of times a month, so the season board would rank hours played and nothing else.
  4. Design Content runs out at about 2.5 hours. The bot made 143 purchases in total and then had nothing left to buy. Idle games keep going because some upgrades never end.
  5. Design Know-how has no late use. After the last variety, know-how only counts as XP, so "spend it before you prestige" stops meaning anything.
  6. Design Cranes leak from play. A game day lasts 9.6 real minutes, so the "daily" tasks pay 3 cranes every 10 minutes and the "weekly" order pays 30 every 67 minutes. Osaka, meant to come from real orders, opens in under an hour.
  7. Design Time away barely counts. The casual player needed almost the same play time as the active one (2.3 hours compared with 2.8). Storage fills quickly, and the foreman does nothing while you are away. Bug The away screen also drops the day counter whenever you are away for less than a full game day.

Iterations

Eleven variants, played to prestige 5

Each variant was patched into the real game and played by the same bot. Every fix that made the economy healthier also made prestiges faster, until the requirement was tied to lifetime prestiges and grew geometrically.

Hours of active play at which each prestige landed. Dots mark prestiges 1 to 5.

Recommendation

The tested set

Played together, these give prestige 1 at 30 minutes, then 54 minutes, 1.6 hours, 3.3 hours and prestige 5 at 8.8 hours for the bot. The casual player gets there in 14.6 days. For a person that probably means 13 to 18 hours of play, or about three weeks of short daily sessions, comfortably inside a monthly season.

1 · Hiring

Price pickers per farm

The price becomes 150 × the farm's price multiplier × 1.45 for each picker past the second on that farm. Every farm can then grow to 14–16 pickers, new regions matter, and putting farms to sleep has a real cost.

2 · Cranes

Make daily and weekly mean real days

Daily tasks reset each real day and the weekly order each real week. Earning Osaka through play then takes about 1 to 2 weeks of daily play, and real orders stay the fast route.

3 · Away time

Keep the foreman working offline

While you are away, the foreman dries and sells lots from whatever the pickers bring in. Storage stays the limit on idle earnings, so upgrading it pays off. The day-counter bug gets fixed at the same time.

4 · Content

Buildings to level 5, plus two upgrades that never end

Farm mastery costs coins and gives +10% picking per level, with the price rising 1.45 times per level. Cupping research costs know-how and gives +0.3 cup score per level, with the price rising 1.4 times per level. Coins and know-how always have somewhere to go, and spending know-how before you prestige matters again.

5 · Prestige

Requirement grows with lifetime prestige

Levels needed become 10, 18, 26, 34, 42, and each level past 10 costs 1.18 times the last. The season board would rank know-how earned this month rather than the number of prestiges, so newer players can compete there. The all-time board stays prestige first. The server's score check would change to match.

A lighter option (levels 10, 18, 26, 34, 42 with each level costing 1.15 times the last) puts prestige 5 at 4.9 hours for the bot, or 8.6 days for the casual player. The tested set still leaves gaps late in a run: about 29 waits of more than 5 minutes for the active bot and 15 for the casual one. Drop events and contracts can fill those, or the upgrade prices can rise more gently.
