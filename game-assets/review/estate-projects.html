Design proposal · 18 October 2026 · late-game uses for each farm's drying area

Estate projects: each farm's drying area becomes a late-game project with its own mechanic

Each farm has a painted area beside its house that does nothing today. The idea is to make it that farm's estate project. It's built up in three stages from farm level 30, it changes the scene as it grows, and each farm adds one new thing to do that comes from its real coffee culture, rather than just another percentage. Each area also gets a showpiece spot that changes with the season.

How it works for every farm

Rule
WhenFrom prestige 4, once a farm reaches farm level 30. Stages two and three need farm levels 40 and 50, so they spread over P4 to P7.
CostCoins and know-how. For example, 2M coins and 50k know-how for stage 1, 10M and 250k for stage 2, 40M and 1M for stage 3, scaled by the farm's cost multiplier. That gives the late-game coin pile and the endless know-how somewhere new to go.
WhereFarm › Fields gets an "Estate project" card for that farm, and tapping the area in the scene opens it.
KeptThrough prestige, like buildings. A dormant farm's project pauses until the farm wakes.
VisualsOverlay sprites over the painted area, like the farmhouse upgrades, plus one small animation per farm (raking, smoke, a boat, running water or koi).

Seasonal showpieces

A new kind of season track reward

Each farm's area gets one showpiece spot, so there are six per farm map. The season track's tier 15 reward (now the season hat) could move to tier 18, with tier 15 becoming that season's showpiece set. It dresses all six farms' spots in the season's theme at once: sakura blossom in April, carp streamers in May, wish strips in July, maple in November. You keep every set you've earned and choose which one is shown, as with building styles. Older sets can be bought from the archive with cranes, like other past-season items.

Why this fits the late-game problems

  • Something to aim for from P4 to P7. There are 18 stages across six farms, each a visible change. They fill the long stretches the 120-hour playtest found.
  • New things to do, not just bigger numbers. Patio drying, micro-lots, a daily ceremony, a visiting trader, barrels that age overnight and koi to feed each give a reason to visit that farm again.
  • Good for idle play. Barrel aging and patio drying both reward time away from the game.
  • On brand. Each project comes from that origin's real coffee culture, which also gives the origin stories (still on hold) a natural place to appear.

Suggested order

  1. The shared system and two projects: the trader's boat at Toba and the barrel cellar at Turrialba. They're the most distinctive and idle-friendly.
  2. Next: the koi pond at Osaka and the ceremony at Gedeo, which are the daily or tap-in moments.
  3. Then: patio drying at Mogiana and micro-lots at Huila.
  4. Last: seasonal showpieces, with art for each of the 12 seasons.
