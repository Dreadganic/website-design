Road to prestige 3: where the start rushes, where it drags

I played three fresh saves to prestige 3 with the bot set to play like a person. It took uneven sessions with long breaks, missed some golden bushes and used only tabs the game had opened. I logged every lesson, pop-up, toast and unlock with its time, and compared the result with how other idle games handle their first hours. Update, 19 October: all four phases are now built. The next section shows the same playthroughs on the new build; the rest of the page describes the game as it was before.

Working
  • The lessons show rather than tell: 9 steps, each a 9–17 word line with an arrow on the thing to tap.
  • Hiring the first picker comes in the first minute, so the first automation is early.
  • A first prestige at about 50 minutes lands on day one.
  • The unlock walks take you to each new thing.
Rushing
  • From level 5 to level 8, eight features arrive in 12 minutes.
  • Each prestige unlocks 4–6 things at once.
  • Every hand sale and every level opens a pop-up.
  • The end of the lessons stacks five pop-ups into about a minute.
Dragging
  • There's a 7.5-minute wait for level 3 with no lesson running.
  • Between prestiges there is nothing new except what the prestige brought.
  • Offline progress tops out after half an hour, so coming back the next day feels thin.
Missing
  • Nothing points at the travel arrows when your second farm opens.
  • Nothing teaches storage filling up while you're away, stamina running out, or Buildings.

The tutorial

Rosa's lessons are the right kind of tutorial. Each step is one action, the line above it is short, the arrow sits on the target, and you can skip. That is the "teach it when they can use it" rule from Celia Hodent's onboarding work, and it is close to how Cookie Clicker and Egg, Inc. start: one obvious tap, then a short list of goals.

The trouble is the clock. Chapter one takes under a minute for the bot and two or three for a person. Then the coffee chapter waits for level 3, so the lessons take 11.5 minutes at the bot's pace and probably 13–15 for someone reading. The average idle-game session is about 8 minutes (GameAnalytics), so many players will close the game during that wait, with the lessons half done.

Involvement: about right. Don't add forced steps. Teach the missing pieces with the unlock walks at the moment each one matters, the way Kolibri fixed Idle Miner Tycoon: playtesters skipped the elevator and warehouse, and a short contextual tip at that chokepoint got more players through.

How much the player is told

Before the first prestige a player sees about 80 pop-ups, besides the sale ones, totalling about 1,900 words. Twelve or thirteen of those are Rosa's cards; most of the rest are celebrations. Selling a lot from the Mill panel opens a pop-up every time until the foreman takes over, which was 78–119 more in the first hour. Toasts run at about 8 a minute all the way to P3.

A better order

Keep the overall length, since prestige 1 at about 50 minutes and prestige 3 at about 2 hours are good. Change the spacing so something new turns up every 5–8 minutes, with no more than three things at a time. Prestige rewards should spread across the first levels of the next run instead of all landing at once.

What's missing

  • Travel between farms. When the second farm opens, the walk ends at the Open button, and nothing points at the › arrow that takes you there. Add one walk step to it.
  • Storage while you're away. Before P1, storage fills after about half an hour away, so a player back the next morning gets 30 minutes' worth. Let early storage hold about 2 hours of picking. On the first welcome back, walk the player to the storage upgrade.
  • Stamina. Running out of hearts shows "tired" over the bush and nothing else. The first time, show one line saying hearts refill over time and on level-up.
  • Buildings. Farm levels and their rewards come from buildings, but the lessons only teach storage. Walk to Buildings the first time one is affordable after the lessons.
  • The first minute. There are two welcome pop-ups back to back, and the second explains cranes, which don't matter for a long while. Keep Rosa's and drop the other. Hold the season-stars message until Ranks opens.

Bugs found on the way

Season stars point to the wrong levelThe first-star toast says the season track "opens with Ranks at level 6". Ranks opens at level 8.
Garbled cupping notebook cardIt reads "That was your first Caturra natural lot went in your cupping notebook." It should read "Your first Caturra natural lot went in your cupping notebook."
Resting farm card can say "level undefined"The card reads the wake-up level 0.4 s after it's queued. In the sim the farm had already woken by then. That's rare in real play, but it's a one-line fix.

What I'd change, in order

A · Quiet the noise

Pop-ups only for real moments: the first sale, unlocking levels, the first contract. One welcome pop-up. Drop the Rosa card wherever a walk already covers it. Fix the three bugs. Trim help boxes to about 20 words, starting with the Mill.

B · Re-space the start

The coffee lesson and Unlock tab at level 2. The farm manager at level 4. Honey at level 7, Shop at 8, Ranks at 9. Weekly tasks after the first prestige.

C · Spread the prestige rewards

Each prestige gives its headline at once, and the rest arrives at levels 4–8 of the new run, so the gap between prestiges gets new things too.

D · Fill the gaps

Walks for the travel arrow, Buildings and storage. A stamina tip. About 2 hours of offline storage before P1.

Each phase can ship on its own. A and the bug fixes are small; B and C change the gates, so I'd rerun these sims after each to check that prestige 1 stays near 50 minutes.

How this was measured

The bot ran three seeds (7, 11, 23) in its "person" mode. It plays sessions of 20–45 minutes with 2–9 hours away between them, misses about 45% of golden bushes, cups about 15% of lots by hand and spends at random. For this review it could only use tabs the game had opened. Times are minutes of play, with breaks left out. The bot doesn't stop to read, so a person will take two to four minutes longer over the first quarter hour. Pop-ups and toasts were counted by hooking the game's own pop-up and toast functions. The bot closes each one at once, so the counts are what a player would be shown, not how long they spend on each. Tab word counts come from rendering each open tab at every level-up.
