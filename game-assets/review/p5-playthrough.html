The first run showed the roastery earning 72% of all coins and maxing its reputation in two hours. After two rounds of rebalancing, it earns about 15% and is still levelling at P5. What we found and fixed

Roastery: far too strong (fixed)

The first P5 runs earned 72% of all coins in the roastery and hit reputation 20 within two hours of unlocking it. Reputation came per kilo, and with 400 kg drums and whole lots fed in, the roastery handled most of the coffee at about twice the green price. Now reputation comes per bag (quality / 10, ×1.5 for the roast a buyer wants, ×2 for blends) on a curve of 100 × (level − 1)². Drums are 40 / 80 / 160 / 320 kg, buyer premiums are ×1.25–1.5, and quality adds up to +20% instead of +60%.

Racing the foreman for lots (fixed)

With a mill foreman, every dry lot was sold the moment it dried, so feeding the roastery meant catching lots before he did. The foreman now keeps the roastery stocked by default, with 2, 4 (the default) or 8 roasts' worth of green coffee waiting, or off. The Mill says what he's doing. Choosing roasts and catching the crack stay yours.

The P4 run feels fuller

That long run went from about 10 hours to 7.5, and from 18–27 stretches of over 10 minutes without a purchase to 3–12. Roastery levels, house blends, notebook stars and building levels 6–7 give it a rhythm: something to buy about every 3 minutes and a new reputation level every 40–70 minutes.

Is there enough content, and does pricing scale?

Content to P5: yes

Every stage now opens something. The map fills in by P3. The roastery takes over as the new goal at P3 to P4, and keeps going past P5 (reputation 12, 15 and 20 still ahead). The notebook is less than a third complete at P5, and the season track runs alongside all of it. The P4 run is still the long one, as it should be in an idle game, but it is no longer empty.

Pricing: mostly good, coins still bunch up late

Up to P3, prices track income closely: purchases every 20–60 seconds and no hoarding. In the P4 run, coins swing between tens of thousands and about a million because building levels 6–7 cost ×3 per level (several million on later farms). Players save for one big upgrade, then spend it all. That's a fair rhythm, but the jumps are steep. Two options if it feels slow:

  • Make building levels past 5 cost ×2 per level instead of ×3, so there's a mid-sized purchase between the big ones.
  • Add a coin sink tied to the roastery, such as roaster upgrades bought with coins alongside the reputation unlocks.
Still worth watching
  • Roasting is the most hands-on part of the late game: a roast every 75–105 seconds, with a crack to catch. That's good for active players. For casual players a head-roaster perk at a high prestige, like Cup all at P2, would keep it from feeling like a chore.
  • The efficient bot reaches P5 in about 11 hours of play. Human-like players reach it in about 10 hours over 4–5 days.
