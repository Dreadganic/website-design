Design audit · 22 October 2026 · cranes, rewards and showing off

Cranes: the right amount, too little to show for them

I checked the crane economy against other idle games and real coffee loyalty programmes, with four questions in mind: more or less currency, more ways to earn, a way to pay money directly, and better cosmetics to show off. The figures come from the game code, the server functions and the earlier playtest bots. People play slower than bots, so read play figures as upper bounds.

Built, 22 October 2026

All three phases are in. Play earnings are unchanged. Anything on the public website waits in the launch kit.

  • Showing off.
    • The server records trophies it can check: Cup of Excellence weeks, Derby podiums, Harvest Day goals, top 10 season finishes and Founding farmer.
    • You pin one beside your name on every board.
    • A trophy shelf and a stack of origin sacks, one per stamp in the stamp's colour, stand in front of each farmhouse. Visitors see both from the server's records.
    • Every launch-season farmer gets the Founding farmer sprout and charm.
  • Achievements. 30 one-time goals worth 290 cranes, in Ranks › Records. The playtest bot earns about 65 by prestige 3, and the rest take weeks to months.
  • Things worth wanting.
    • Seven animated farm effects, two at a time: Fireflies, Butterflies, Morning mist, Sakura petals, Maple leaves, Crane pair (80–150 cranes) and Hanabi, for subscribers only. Visitors see them move.
    • A monthly market stall of three colourways (15–30 cranes) from 36, each back in the same month next year.
    • Visitors can look into your roastery.
  • Coffee pays more.
    • The month's first order pays 30 cranes more, and subscription renewals pay 10 more.
    • Every linked order brings a gift code. A friend who opens it gets a furoshiki bundle for their farm, and you get 10 cranes.
    • The thank-you card and a new line on each coffee's page explain this, ready in launch/.

The four answers

More or less currency?About right in total

Don't cut play again. In a small community the free players are the community. The order side is what's too small: for an engaged player a ¥3,000 bag is worth less than a week of play. Add a first-order-of-the-month bonus so a monthly bag counts double.

More ways to earn?A few, one-time only

No new weekly sources. Add one-time achievements (about 300 cranes in all) that reward new players early without raising the weekly rate for veterans, and a free Founding farmer charm for the launch season.

Pay money for cranes?No: sell coffee that brings cranes

Selling cranes would earn little from a small community, would bring in prepaid-payment rules, and would put a yen price on every crane given with an order. Every paid route should go through coffee: monthly bonus, subscriptions, gift orders and seasonal coffee sets.

Better cosmetics to show off?Yes, the main gap

Most of what players earn can't be seen by anyone else. Badges stay on the device, the roastery is private, and nothing on the farm shows which real coffees you've had. Make the farm visit the showroom, and add animated pieces as the top crane tier.

In these games, premium currency is scarce for free players because it is the product being sold. Beju sells coffee, so cranes work more like loyalty points with a game attached. That's why the comparison below matters more.

Two lessons carry over. Real coffee is earned by buying, at roughly 4–9% back. Gifting is unusually popular: half of Blue Bottle members choose to treat someone else. A gift order that gives both people something in the game would suit Beju's small community and bring in new players.

Why not sell cranes

  • Little money. Most players in free games never pay, and Beju's community will be small. A ¥3,000 bag earns far more than any likely crane sales would.
  • It changes what cranes mean. Today a crane says "I drink Beju coffee" or "I play every day". Once they're for sale, they just mean "I paid".
  • Prepaid-payment rules. Paid game currency counts as a prepaid payment instrument under the Payment Services Act. If the unused paid balance ever passes ¥10 million on 31 March or 30 September, Beju must notify the Local Finance Bureau within two months, deposit at least half of that balance, and refund players if the game closes. Below that line nothing is filed, but paid and free cranes still have to be kept apart from day one.
  • It puts a price on every crane. While cranes can't be bought, those given with an order have no clear yen value. Once they have a shop price, every crane given with coffee becomes a valued premium (see the legal points below).
  • App stores later. If the game ever ships as an app, digital purchases fall under Apple and Google's rules and fees. Since December 2025, Japan's smartphone competition law lets developers offer other payment methods, but Apple's own in-app purchase must be offered alongside, with fees of 5–26%. Physical coffee is outside those rules.

The things that are hardest to earn are the ones nobody else sees: a Cup win, a Derby pennant, a full passport. In the games that sell cosmetics well, the best items are seen where other players already look, are tied to a moment or an achievement, and move. Beju's pixel farm is almost entirely still, so a few small animations would stand out a lot.

Proposals

  • Trophy shelf on the farm. A decoration that shows your best three badges to visitors. Only badges the server can check count (Cup, Derby, Harvest Day, season ranks), so they're stored on the server, not just on the device.
  • Origin sacks. A stack of jute sacks by the farmhouse, one printed sack for each origin you've ordered. It shows visitors which real coffees you drink, and it advertises the shop at the same time.
  • Pinned badges on board rows. One badge next to your name on every board, so a Cup winner is recognised everywhere.
  • Animated pieces as the top crane tier. Fireflies at dusk, falling sakura, chimney steam from the roastery, swaying lanterns, a crane that crosses the farm now and then, maple leaves in autumn. 80–150 cranes each, above the 30–80 for still building styles.
  • A monthly market stall. Three crane items a month (recolours of building styles and decorations, 15–30 each), each coming back a year later so nobody feels they have to rush.
  • A look into the roastery. Let visitors see one screen of your roastery, so the 33 roastery cosmetics count too.
  • Founding farmer. A free charm for everyone who plays in the launch season. A small community makes it rare and gives early players something to keep.

With about 600 cranes of animated pieces and the market stall taking about 75 a month, an engaged player needs about a year to own the permanent shop, longer if they also buy from the stall. After that the stall and the season archive take about 175 a month against about 150 earned, so cranes keep their value.

Points to check with a lawyer before launch

Not legal advice · from public guidance, to confirm
  • The 100 g samples and the 20% cap. Under the Premiums and Representations Act, a premium given to everyone who buys (総付景品) can be worth at most 20% of the purchase, so ¥600 on a ¥3,000 order. A 100 g bag worth ¥1,400–2,250, redeemed with a ¥3,000 order, would be far over that if it counts as a premium. There are three ways it may not count. The guidance exempts genuine samples (見本) of a sensible size, which suggests a smaller taster. It treats a discount, or more of the same product at the same price, as a discount rather than a premium, which suggests "100 g extra of a coffee in your order". Or the minimum order could rise. The Consumer Affairs Agency is also looking at raising the caps, possibly from 2027.
  • Cranes given with orders. Cosmetics that can't be bought anywhere have no clear market price, which keeps their value as a premium low. Selling cranes would change that.
  • Selling cranes or gift cards. Both are prepaid payment instruments: the ¥10 million line on 31 March and 30 September, a deposit of half the unused balance above it, and fines for not filing. Instruments usable for six months or less are exempt.
  • No paid random boxes. Since July 2012, "complete the set" rewards from paid random draws (コンプガチャ) are banned as card matching. The crane shop sells each item directly, so keep it that way. Prizes by chance tied to purchases are prize draws with their own limits.
  • Gift orders and referrals. Check whether a reward to the person who refers counts as a premium on their own purchase.

Proposed changes

All three phases were built on 22 October 2026 (see the top of this page). Anything on the live site is kept separate until launch.

Phase 1 · showing off (game and server)
  • The server stores the badges it can check, and players pin one to their board row.
  • A trophy shelf and origin sacks on the farm, shown on visits.
  • The Founding farmer charm for the launch season.
  • About 30 one-time achievements worth about 300 cranes in all, most of them in the first two months.
Phase 2 · things worth wanting (game and art)
  • Six animated pieces at 80–150 cranes.
  • The monthly market stall with recolours.
  • A look into the roastery on farm visits.
Phase 3 · coffee pays more (server ready, site kept separate)
  • +30 cranes for the first order of each month, checked on the server when an order is claimed.
  • +10 per subscription renewal, plus the subscriber piece.
  • Gift orders that give the friend a welcome cosmetic.
  • Product-page text and thank-you-page links waiting for launch day.

Play earnings stay as they are. Today's cranes and purchases stay valid, and nothing is taken from anyone.

For launch

  • Ask a lawyer about the points above, starting with sample size against the 20% cap, before samples are promised to anyone.
  • On launch day, launch/apply.py adds the "Add this order to Beju Farm" card to the thank-you page and the new line to each coffee's page. Without the card, no order earns cranes.
  • Still open from before: Stripe test events, the 100 g sample bags, a test order on a preview with GAME_SAMPLES=on, SEASON_FIRST and SEASON_JOIN in both files, a look at the estate art and the moved cupping table, your own first 10 minutes of play, a co-op test on a preview (stickers, help, a Cup attempt, farm visits), and a decision on November's Harvest Day (Saturday 7 November is the default).
