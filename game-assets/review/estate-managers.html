Five estate managers, one for each estate's chore

The single estate manager becomes five specialists, each tied to one estate's daily chore, with their own look, their own scene and their own training. Each one takes over a job you've already been doing yourself, and does it a little worse than you. Huila is the one estate without a manager, because its micro-lots come by chance and there's no chore to hand over.

The same rules for all five

Do it yourself firstA manager can be hired only after you've done that chore yourself a few times. It gives each estate time to breathe.
Never as good as youEach works at 40% of your result, rising 5% per training level to 90% at level 10.
You get first goThey step in only after you've had a fair chance, measured in minutes of play, so your own tap always comes first.
No stars, no tasksTheir work earns no season stars, counts for no daily or weekly task and gives no cranes.

The five managers

Terreiro foreman

Mogiana, Brazil
Your chore
new Rake the patio. From stage 1, tap Rake on the estate (or the patio in the scene) once every 2 hours. Natural lots started in the next 30 minutes cup +0.5, because they dry evenly. It gives Mogiana a tap-in moment like the others.
He does
Rakes if you haven't 20 minutes after it's ready, for +0.2 cup untrained, up to +0.45 trained.
Hire when
You've raked 5 times.
Look
White shirt and straw hat, raking the rows on the patio.

Ceremony host

Gedeo, Ethiopia
Your chore
Hold the daily coffee ceremony (stage 2): golden bushes twice as often and +20% picking, for 30 minutes (45 at stage 3).
She does
Holds it if you haven't after 30 minutes of play that day, for 40% to 90% of the time.
Hire when
You've held 3 ceremonies.
Look
A white shawl with a green and gold border, pouring from the jebena at the hut.

Harbour master

Lake Toba, Sumatra
Your chore
Fill the trader's boat order (stage 1): it pays about 2.5× the market, and sometimes cranes.
He does
When the boat calls, he sets matching dry lots aside so the foreman doesn't sell them, asks the foreman to process what the boat wants, and delivers. But he haggles worse: his deliveries earn 40% to 90% of the boat's premium over the market, and never the cranes.
Hire when
You've filled 2 boat orders.
Look
An ulos cloth in red, black and white, waving the boat in from the jetty.

Cellar master

Turrialba, Costa Rica
Your chore
Lay lots down in the barrel cellar (stage 2) and take them out at +3 cup.
He does
Keeps the barrels full with the most valuable dry lots, and takes his out at +1 cup untrained, up to +2.5 trained. He leaves lots you laid down alone.
Hire when
You've aged 2 lots fully.
Look
A cream shirt and red bandana, rolling a barrel at the cellar door.

Koi keeper

Osaka garden
Your chore
Feed the koi every 3 hours (stage 1): crack windows +1 second for an hour (+2 for 2 hours at stage 3), and sometimes a golden koi with cranes.
She does
Feeds them 20 minutes after they're ready, for 40% to 90% of the crack bonus, and never finds a golden koi.
Hire when
You've fed the koi 5 times.
Look
An indigo happi coat, scattering food from the red bridge.

Where they live in the game

  • On the estate card: once a manager can be hired, the estate's own card offers it, next to the chore it takes over. Once hired, it shows what they did today ("Raked at 14:20, +0.2 cup").
  • Farm › Staff: an "Estate managers" section lists all five: locked ones say what opens them, and each hired one has its training row.
  • In the scenes: each manager stands at their own estate doing their chore, drawn over the estate art in that estate's colours, so a built-up estate looks staffed.
  • Introductions: when a manager becomes hireable, Rosa says one line about them, once.
  • The head roaster stays as he is.

Decisions for you

1. Is Huila the right estate to leave without a manager?

Its micro-lots come by chance, so there's no chore to hand over. The alternative is to invent a Huila chore, for example grading honey and barrel lots at the greenhouse to spot micro-lots, and leave Mogiana passive instead.

I'd suggest leaving Huila out and giving Mogiana the rake. Mogiana is the first estate most players build, so its chore and foreman teach the idea early.
2. Should the managers have personal names, like Rosa?

For example, Zé on the patio, Almaz at the ceremony, Tigor at the harbour, Mario in the cellar and Yuki at the koi pond.

I'd suggest yes. Rosa shows how much a name adds, and names make them read as characters rather than upgrades. You may want to choose or check the names yourself.
3. Should the boat order's cranes stay yours alone?I'd suggest yes. Cranes tie to real orders and active play, so no staff member should earn them.
