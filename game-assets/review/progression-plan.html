Design research · 18 October 2026

Too few unlocks, all bought with one currency, so the whole game opens in two hours

Beju Farm has about 18 real gameplay unlocks, and almost all of them cost know-how, the same currency that grows fastest. A player sees all five farms, every process and eight of nine coffees within about 110 minutes of play. The games we compare against have far more to unlock, gate it by more than one thing, and add whole new systems as you go. The fix is to gate unlocks by farm progress, add depth to what already exists, then add a new layer for the mid-game.

Where we are

~18gameplay unlocks: 8 coffees, 4 processes, 4 farms, plus Osaka
~110 minof play until all of them are open (human-like bot)
1main currency gating them: know-how

After those first two hours, the remaining goals are the prestige perks, building levels 6–10, farm milestones, the season track, Geisha and the Osaka garden. They're good goals, but they're mostly the same kind of thing: a bigger number on something you already have.

One idle-game postmortem puts a good first prestige at 15–35 minutes, and ours is 20–33. The first run is fine. Everything after it is where we compress.

Where we're misaligned

1 · Breadth

18 unlocks against dozens or hundreds. Even well paced, a small list runs out.

2 · One gate

Coffees, processes and farms all cost know-how, and know-how income roughly doubles every few levels. Prices that look far apart (150 up to 60,000) collapse into the same hour. The other games gate by level, objectives or collections, which don't speed up with income.

3 · Thin depth per unlock

Each coffee and process is mostly a multiplier. There's some real choice in contracts, terroir and cupping, but nothing that makes you learn a coffee or come back to an old one.

4 · No new layer after P3

Egg, Inc. adds contracts and artifacts, Idle Miner adds continents, Stardew adds the island. After our first two hours, the game gets bigger but not different.

The plan

A
Gate unlocks by farm progressSmall to medium · re-paces what exists
  • Farms need the previous farm built up: Huila needs Mogiana at farm level 5; Gedeo, Huila at 10 (plus P1); Toba, Gedeo at 15 (plus P2); Turrialba, Toba at 20 (plus P3). Farm levels come from buildings, so coins finally matter for progress, and know-how stops being the only key.
  • Coffees come from their farms: each coffee can only be unlocked once a farm where it's native is open. That means Pink Bourbon from Huila, Heirloom from Gedeo, Catimor from Toba and Geisha from Turrialba or Osaka. Opening a farm then means new coffees, not just more land.
  • Processes come from farm milestones: the barrels from Huila at farm level 10, strawberry infused from Toba at level 10. They still unlock straight away when you order that coffee on bejucoffee.com.
B
Depth on what's already thereMedium · long-term goals without new art
  • Cupping notebook: a collection log for every coffee and process pair (up to 54 entries). The first lot, 25 lots and 250 lots each earn a star, a small cup bonus for that pair, and titles for completing a coffee or a process. Old coffees get a reason to come back, and completionists get months of goals.
  • Regular buyers: each contract buyer remembers you. Their loyalty levels unlock their premium contracts, a recurring order and a thank-you postcard for the passport.
  • Origin knowledge: farm milestones teach a real fact about that origin's coffee, in Rosa's voice. Players learn about the coffee they can buy, which is the brand's whole point.
C
A new layer for the mid-game: the roasteryLarge · new system, opens around P3 or the end of week 1
  • A roasting stage after the mill: green coffee becomes light, medium or dark roasts, each suiting different coffees and different Japanese buyers (kissaten, specialty cafés, convenience-store blends).
  • Its own upgrades (roasters, cooling trays, a cupping lab) and its own orders. The farms stay the engine, and the roastery is where you make your name.
  • It mirrors what Beju actually does, roasting in Japan. It's also the natural home for the Osaka garden and for crossplay rewards.
D
Room to grow laterOngoing · seasons and launches
  • New origins as seasonal expansions, for example origins Beju may stock later, each with its own natives, process and milestones.
  • A second, longer prestige layer once players reach P8, if the data shows they want it.

I'd start with A: it re-paces the game we have with small code changes, and the bots can measure it straight away. B adds the long-term goals with no new art. C is the big one and deserves its own design pass first.
