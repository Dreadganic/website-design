Design brief · 19 October 2026 · community, co-op and an endgame for the all-time board

Playing together, and a reason to keep playing once the farm runs itself

Beju Farm is a solo game today. The weekly team order uses three made-up farmers, and the all-time board ranks prestige, then level, so it mostly rewards time spent. This brief looks at how other games handle community, co-op and late-game competition, then proposes four community features and one new endgame mode. Nothing here is built yet.

What this teaches:

  • Pooled goals in small groups, matched by strength, are the proven co-op loop for idle and farming games.
  • Look-but-don't-touch visits are cheap to build and well loved.
  • For fair late-game competition, the strongest pattern is a fixed-seed challenge where everyone gets the same conditions, ranked weekly with a lasting hall of fame.
  • No game I found ranks players on a new activity only once they've fully automated, so that would be a fresh idea for Beju Farm.

Community and co-op: four proposals

1. Farm visits

small · first

Each farm gets a short farm code and a Visit button on the leaderboard. Visiting shows a read-only snapshot of that farm: its scene, estates, farmhouse, decor, showpiece and farmer. Visitors can leave a Cheers stamp, one per farm per day, and farms show their Cheers count. A small season star cap for leaving Cheers rewards visiting without letting it be farmed.

It's like Animal Crossing's dream addresses. It gives the cosmetics and estates an audience, and needs only a stored snapshot on the server, which already keeps looks.

2. Cooperatives

medium · the core co-op feature

Players join a cooperative of up to 8. It's how real coffee farmers group up, so it fits the brand.

  • The weekly co-op order replaces today's team order, which uses made-up farmers. The co-op gets a pooled order, for example 40,000 kg of washed Heirloom at cup 88+. Every member's matching lots count, and rewards come in tiers by how far the co-op gets.
  • Fair matching as in Egg, Inc.: each co-op's order size scales with its members' strength, so a new co-op and a veteran one both have a fair goal.
  • Help without chat: members can tap Help on a co-op mate's drying lot to speed it up a little, once a day per mate, like Hay Day's help requests. Messages are preset stickers ("Nice roast!", "Need washed lots!"), not free chat, so there's nothing to moderate.
  • The co-op ledger shows each member's share this week, the order's progress and the co-op's badge.

Unlocks with the Daily tab, after Rosa's lessons. Joining is by invite code or "find a co-op" matching players of similar prestige.

3. Co-op Derby

medium · after co-ops

A weekly league for co-ops, like the Hay Day Derby. Each co-op is grouped with about 10 co-ops of similar strength, and members complete tasks from a shared board: roast 20 dark bags, deliver 5 contracts, fill the boat. Five leagues move co-ops up and down each week, and higher leagues give more cranes, a co-op badge and a cosmetic. It gives co-ops something to aim for beyond the weekly order.

4. Harvest Day: a monthly community goal

small · ties in the shop

Once a month, every player works towards one global target over a weekend, for example "Together, roast one million bags of Sumatra Strawberry", with a live progress bar.

  • Hitting it unlocks something for everyone: a decoration in game, and in the real world a discount code or a limited roast on bejucoffee.com.
  • It reuses the existing drop events, giving them a shared goal.
  • Like Pokémon GO's Community Day, it's a date people plan around, without needing everyone online at the same hour.

A new endgame: the Cup of Excellence

The concept

Once a farm runs itself, the all-time board should rank skill, not hours. Real coffee has the answer: the Cup of Excellence, a competition where farms submit their best lot and judges cup them blind. In Beju Farm it becomes a weekly championship on a fresh competition plot. Everyone gets the same seed, coffee, weather and buyers, with no staff, upgrades or estates. Only how well you play counts.

How a week works

  1. Unlock: when every coffee, process and farm is open and the main staff are hired (about prestige 7). A one-time card from Rosa: "You don't need me any more. Time to see how good you really are."
  2. The brief: every Monday, the same fixed-seed challenge for everyone. For example: "a small Huila plot with Pink Bourbon and Caturra, rainy season, a café buyer wanting light roasts, 25 minutes of play".
  3. Play it: pick by hand, choose processes, cup by hand, roast and catch every crack, then blend and sell. The run can be split into sittings, and a run can be retried twice a week, with the best one counting.
  4. Scoring: a cup score for your best lot and a value score for everything sold, combined into one championship score. The weekly board shows the top 100, your rank and your percentile.
  5. The rating: each week's placing feeds a lasting Q grader rating, which becomes the new all-time board, the Hall of Excellence. It rises with good weeks and slowly fades if you stop competing, so it stays current.

Why this fits

  • It's the only thing in the late game that rewards active play over automation, and it uses the skills the game already teaches: catching cracks, cupping and choosing processes.
  • Fixed seeds make it fair, as Diablo's Gauntlet showed: a veteran with 300 hours and a newer player at prestige 7 race on equal terms.
  • It's short and weekly, so it adds a reason to come back without becoming a chore.
  • It's on brand. Beju could feature the weekly winner's "lot" on the site, or send monthly champions a real bag of coffee.

What changes about the all-time board

  • Hall of Excellence (new, the headline board): ranks the Q grader rating.
  • Legacy (today's all-time board, renamed): ranks prestige and level. It still honours time spent, but it's no longer the only measure.
  • Season and Rookies stay as they are.

The hard part: keeping it honest

The game runs on the player's device, so a championship score can be faked. The fixed seed helps: the server knows exactly what the plot started with. The game sends the run's actions, and the server replays them to check the score, so a score the actions can't produce is rejected. This is the largest piece of work here, and it matters most if real prizes are involved. Real prizes may also bring legal rules on contests and sweepstakes that vary by country, so check before offering any.

Suggested order

  1. Farm visits and Harvest Day. Small, no group play needed, and they give the cosmetics and the shop a community face.
  2. Cooperatives with the weekly co-op order. The core co-op loop, replacing the made-up team order.
  3. Cup of Excellence. The endgame. Start with weekly boards and the rating, without real prizes, then add server replay checks before any prizes.
  4. Co-op Derby, once co-ops have enough players to fill leagues.

Co-ops and the Derby only work with enough players: a league of 10 co-ops needs a few hundred active players. Until then, the monthly community goal and farm visits carry the community side. They work with any number of players.

Decisions for you

1. Chat in co-ops: stickers only, or free text?I'd suggest stickers only. It avoids moderation and keeps the game safe for all ages.
2. Real-world prizes for Cup of Excellence champions?I'd suggest featuring winners on the site and in the game at first, and adding real coffee prizes later, once the server checks and any legal review are done.
3. Which one should be built first?I'd suggest farm visits, then Harvest Day: the quickest wins, and both work from launch day with few players.
